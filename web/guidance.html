Read the detailed explanation

Clearcost helps an energy operations reviewer compare a contractor’s invoice with agreed charging rules and recorded meter-service visits. It highlights differences, presents supporting evidence and records the reviewer’s conclusion.

The demonstration contains five fictional contractor agreements, seventy-five visit records and eighty invoice lines. Deliberately planted issues include incorrect rates, cancellation charges, incomplete visits and duplicate charges. The initial checks flag twenty lines with a combined known difference of £987 before VAT.

A finding is a question to investigate. A flagged difference is not automatically a confirmed error, and a confirmed error is not money recovered.

Where does the language model fit? Saved extraction drafts provide a second reading of contract terms, with supporting passages and page links. They help a person inspect the evidence. They do not currently determine calculated amounts.

Understand the identifiers and supporting data

A contractor provides services. A contract is the agreement covering those services. A job identifies one visit. An invoice line is one charge, so a duplicated job can appear on more than one line. One contract extraction can therefore support many visits.

Match records using the contractor, contract ID, job ID and invoice-line ID together—not by the amount or the folder number. The expected-results JSON files are test answers only. Synthetic geography separately links service areas to contracts and visits.

“Valid charge” does not mean “the application worked correctly.” It means the charge itself is justified. A note saying “checked” does not complete an inspection while its status remains “Needs review”.

Progress counts completed inspections among flagged invoice lines. All contractors gives the overall picture; a selected contractor has its own progress. Reopening a decision as “Needs review” or “Awaiting evidence” reduces completion accordingly.

Saving, returning tomorrow and conflicting edits

Save before closing. Your current decision and history are stored in the local database and loaded when the same workspace is restarted. Original CSVs and PDFs stay unchanged.

Saving an unchanged review again does not add a duplicate. A changed decision adds history and supersedes the earlier decision. If another window saves a different review first, your stale save is rejected; refresh and inspect the latest record before trying again.

When changing filters or findings with unsaved notes, choose “Keep editing” to retain them or “Discard changes” to leave them behind. If the server is unavailable, displayed summaries may be out of date.

Read the detailed explanation

Evidence-linked reconciliation, human review with history, contractor analysis, synthetic geographic coverage and model-assisted contract reading. The local implementation has automated calculation and HTTP tests plus browser workflow checks.

  • Controlled data: the calculation parser supports the supplied synthetic contract templates. General PDF uploads and arbitrary contract wording are not yet an end-to-end supported workflow.
  • Drafts remain advisory: approving extracted terms and using them to recalculate invoices is not yet implemented.
  • Inspection is not financial resolution: confirming an error does not amend an invoice, request a credit note, contact a contractor or recover funds.
  • Local review storage: reviewers share the running demo’s database; typed reviewer names are not authenticated accounts. Public hosting and its access/persistence checks are still pending.
  • Future integrations: Google Sheets synchronisation and the supplier correction/credit-note workflow are not included yet.

For an audit of this demonstration, assess the accuracy of the evidence links, the clarity of the decisions and the consistency of progress across views. The synthetic sample does not establish any real contractor’s performance.

About this demonstration

Introduction to the Application

A portfolio demonstration of energy contractor cost reconciliation, created by Lucy Nowacki.

What the application does

Clearcost helps a reviewer answer a practical question: were we charged what the agreement allows? It compares a contractor’s invoice lines with agreed prices and visit records, flags possible billing errors, and brings the supporting evidence together for human inspection.

What is in the demonstration

The dataset is entirely synthetic: five contractors, five agreements, seventy-five visits and eighty invoice lines. It includes completed, cancelled and incomplete visits, approved exceptions and deliberately planted billing errors. The initial checks identify twenty flagged lines with a combined difference of £987 before VAT. This amount is a discrepancy to investigate, not money recovered.

How to use it

Start with Data analysis to compare contractors and plan the work. Geography shows the fictional service areas. Open Invoice review, choose a contractor and a finding, then compare the billed amount, expected amount and difference. Check the visit record, contract and any relevant supporting evidence before recording a conclusion.

Choose Needs review if the inspection is unfinished, or Awaiting evidence if information is missing. Choose Confirmed error when the evidence supports a billing error, or Valid charge when it justifies the charge. Add your name and a clear reason, then save. The latest saved decision becomes current; earlier decisions remain in Review history. Progress shows completed inspections across the dataset and for the selected contractor.

Where the language model helps

Contract extraction drafts contain saved model-extracted terms and supporting quotations. One draft covers one agreement and can support the review of many visits. The draft is a reading aid: reviewers must check its meaning against the source. Current calculations use the controlled contract parser, not model-generated terms. Browsing this demonstration makes no live model calls.

What is saved, and what remains outside the demo

Python performs the checks and serves the application; browser JavaScript displays the findings, charts and map. Saved decisions and history are stored in a local SQLite database, so work can continue after restarting. Original source CSVs and PDFs stay unchanged.

Completing an inspection does not correct an invoice, contact a supplier, request a credit note or recover money. The current demo supports its supplied contract templates. General uploads, approved model terms driving calculations, Google Sheets integration and public hosting are not yet implemented. Reviewers share the local database; typed reviewer names are not authenticated accounts.
